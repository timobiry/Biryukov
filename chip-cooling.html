Direct-to-Substrate Dual Jet Impingement for SiC Power Modules

Double-Sided Liquid Cooling Architecture, ANSYS Fluent CFD & Experimental TSEP Validation

41% Reduction in Thermal Resistance (0.164 vs. 0.278 K/W)
61°C Peak Junction Temp Drop at 250 W (103°C vs. 164°C)
586 W/cm² Extrapolated Heat Flux Capacity at 150°C Limit
>2× Localized Heat Transfer Coefficient at Hotspots

1. Project Overview & Manifold Architecture

Increasing power density in medium-voltage (MV) Silicon Carbide (SiC) devices creates severe, localized heat fluxes that overwhelm conventional channel-flow cold plates. This project developed a dual-sided, direct-to-substrate jet-impingement (DJI) cooling system for a 3.3 kV / 100 A double-sided-cooled (DSC) SiC phase-leg module, eliminating both cold plates and thermal interface materials (TIMs).

  • Asymmetric Hotspot Targeting: The DSC module contains two SiC dies bonded by their drain sides to opposing substrates and linked vertically via source-side conductive posts, generating two distinct hotspots per exterior substrate.
  • Resin 3D-Printed Manifolds: Custom manifolds clamp directly onto opposing module substrates, routing fluid through a 7 mm inlet channel into two 1.75 mm diameter nozzles positioned at a 3.5 mm nozzle-to-substrate standoff (H/D = 2).
  • Symmetric Hydraulic Routing: Submerged jets impinge perpendicularly on the substrate hotspots, transition into radial wall jets, and exit upward through a central port—balancing pressure drop and minimizing wall-jet collision losses.

2. ANSYS Fluent CFD & Thermal Simulation

Both the proposed DJI manifold and a conventional 7 mm channel-flow baseline were simulated in ANSYS Fluent under single-device (250 W) and dual-device (250 W per chip) heating conditions at a total flow rate of 2 L/min (1 L/min per side).

  • Boundary Layer Disruption: High-velocity impinging jets disrupt the stagnation-zone thermal boundary layer, delivering more than twice the local convective heat transfer coefficient of channel flow directly over the semiconductor hotspots.
  • 35% Peak Temperature Rise Reduction: At 250 W dissipation, CFD predicted a peak junction temperature of 107°C for DJI versus 164°C for channel flow.
  • Uniform Parallel Cooling: Because the jets cool each die in parallel, the DJI architecture eliminates the inlet-to-outlet thermal gradient typical of serial channel flow, reducing thermo-mechanical stress across the module.

3. Closed-Loop Hydraulic Test Bench & Python TSEP Analysis

A closed-loop hydraulic test rig was constructed using a positive displacement pump to circulate deionized water at a calibrated 1 L/min per manifold across heating loads from 25 W to 250 W.

Vf(T) = Vf(Tref) + k(T − Tref)
  • TSEP Sensing: Steady-state junction temperature was measured via the SiC body-diode forward voltage drop (Vf) as a Temperature-Sensitive Electrical Parameter (TSEP) using a fast-switching 50 mA sensing current.
  • Python Extrapolation Pipeline: A custom Python post-processing script filters out the initial 5 ms of electrical switching transients and extrapolates the linear root-mean-square time (√t) cooling curve back to power interruption (t = 0).

4. Experimental Thermal Performance

Experimental validation closely matched CFD predictions across the entire 25 W – 250 W operating envelope at 1 L/min per side, demonstrating substantial thermal headroom for high-power-density SiC packaging:
